Membership Economics · 6 min · full textOriginal
Why I picked this: you said you've been wondering how Costco makes money, and usage-based pricing is on your mind.

A membership store makes most of its profit from the fee, not from what's on the shelves. Prices stay close to cost, so members keep coming back, and coming back is what renews the fee.

That flips the usual question. Instead of asking how much margin each sale can carry, it asks what makes someone renew. Mostly, it's trust that the price is fair.

What it means for a SaaS founder

Summary of this sectionClaim: the fee is the relationship. Evidence: renewals carry the profit. For you: keep usage pricing predictable and make renewal the obvious choice.

If you charge a fee with usage on top, the fee is the relationship. Keep usage pricing boring and predictable, and spend your effort making the renewal feel obvious.

The best renewal pitch is a year of not being surprised. Every surprise on an invoice is a reason to reconsider the whole relationship.

A founder's blog · via X · 9 min · full textOriginal

Our first B2B contract was priced in a spreadsheet with three columns: what it cost us, what the customer said they'd save, and what we were scared to ask for.

We picked the middle number. In hindsight it should have been the scary one: the customer signed in a day, which told us everything about where we'd landed.

What we'd change

Summary of this sectionClaim: they underpriced. Evidence: a one-day signature. For you: anchor on the value the customer names, not your costs.

Price from the savings the customer names, not from your costs. Put the number in writing early, and treat a fast yes as information rather than a win.
